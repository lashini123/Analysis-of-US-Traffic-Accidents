## Key Insights from Chicago Traffic Crash Analysis

### 1. Long-Term Trends

* Traffic crash volumes exhibit noticeable variation across years rather than remaining constant, indicating that external factors such as infrastructure development and policy changes may influence accident patterns.

### 2. Seasonal Patterns

* Monthly crash trends show clear seasonality, with higher accident frequencies during certain months, suggesting the influence of weather, travel behavior, and traffic density.

### 3. Weather Conditions

* The majority of crashes occur during clear weather conditions, indicating that adverse weather is not the primary driver of crash frequency. Human behavior and traffic congestion likely play a greater role.

### 4. Lighting Conditions

* Daylight conditions dominate crash occurrences, reinforcing the idea that high traffic volume periods pose more risk than reduced visibility alone.

### 5. Speed and Injury Severity

* Higher posted speed limits are associated with increased total injury counts, confirming speed as a critical factor in crash severity.

### 6. Injury Distribution and Outliers

* Most crashes result in low injury counts; however, a small number of extreme cases contribute disproportionately to total injuries, highlighting the importance of targeting high-severity incidents.

### 7. Crash Type Patterns

* Certain crash types consistently appear more frequently, pointing to structural traffic system issues rather than random events.
